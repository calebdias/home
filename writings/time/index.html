Managing Time with Purpose

Caleb S. Dias: September 6, 2026

Outlined below is the time management system I developed to keep my days balanced and peaceful. Perhaps someone else will find it useful. The system is hierarchical, and broken down into four parts. Each part is influenced by the one above it.

Part 1: Statement of Purpose

This is the governing level of the system, upon which everything else hangs. It’s a one-page document outlining the detailed, yet fundamental reasons for my existence. Before I can manage my time, I need to understand the ultimate reasons behind my actions. For me, those reasons are deeply rooted in my faith in Jesus Christ (Statement of Faith and Purpose). You can make this your own. I view this document as an anchor which brings meaning into the systems beneath it.

Part 2: The Daily Resolution

Knowing my core values, I wrote down what an ideal day looks like. This isn’t about planning every detail, but rather the core structure. If I were writing a book, this would be the outline. I took inspiration from Benjamin Franklin’s daily schedule, and modified it to fit me. Obviously, yours may look different than mine, and not everyone will be able to create an ideal schedule. Still, it's a good idea to take inventory of the flexibility one does have and start there.

Also, the Daily Resolution might change slightly over time as life progresses. The important thing is that any adjustments stay in line with the Statement of Purpose, which governs.

Here's mine, for reference:

5:00 AM – 7:00 AM: Rise, exercise, and pray. Wash and breakfast. Study and creative thinking.

Each day begins with prayer. I thank God and ask for His guidance. My energy levels and cognitive functions are best in the morning, so I also make a point to think creatively at that time. This doesn’t mean I’m not creative at other points in the day. Remember, this is an ideal structure, not a rigid minute-by-minute schedule. I view the Daily Resolution as a compass which keeps me on track, meaning there is grace for failure.

7:00 AM – 12:00 PM: Review the day’s business and begin work.

I take out my Action List (more on that later) and review my plan for the day. Once I deem the plan reasonable, I begin work with the most energy-intensive projects first.

12:00 PM – 1:00 PM: Read, overlook my accounts, or time with my family. Dine.

Lunch time. I typically go home for lunch, but not always. I take this time to eat, review what I’ve accomplished so far, and unwind a bit. I may also help my wife so she can catch a break as well.

1:00 PM – 6:00 PM: Routine work and plan tomorrow’s business.

Back to work. Toward the afternoon, my energy levels begin to decline. Because of that, I’ll work on less taxing, routine projects. When the day’s work is concluded, I’ll take out my Action List (again, more on that later) and plan out the next day. I’ll also review my calendar and make any needed modifications.

6:00 PM – 10:00 PM: Supper. Love and serve my family. Pray in solitude.

Supper time is sacred. My family sits together at the table, and spends time together. Afterward, we’ll clean up and enjoy the evening. We may go out, or stay home. Around 9:00 PM, I spend time alone (usually outside) praying and thinking. I’ll ponder the question “What good have I done this day?”, then journal, and go to bed – usually around 10:00 PM.

Part 3: The Calendar

My wife and I use a shared calendar system (a CalDAV server) which I connect to from my laptop, and she from her phone. It's a standard setup - even a paper calendar would work. The difference is that now the Daily Resolution provides a framework to build out our weeks while staying consistent to our purpose and values.

I review the calendar once a day before suppertime, adding items I’ve jotted down throughout the day from my Action List. This frees me from being tied down to the calendar, as I don’t have a smartphone. I consider that an upside, but using a calendar on a smartphone would be work too.

Part 4: The Action List

This is a small notebook I keep with me at all times. I’ll write down things to do that day, such as “Mow the lawn” or items to put on my calendar such as “Schedule time to visit the Martins”.

Things that I plan to do that day, I will put a small dot next to. When they’re done, I cross them off. The next day, during my calendar session, I’ll add and offload items to and from my Action List.

If something requires a specific time slot on the calendar - like an appointment or a scheduled visit - I transfer it over to the shared calendar so my wife and I can both see it. Everything else, like quick chores or flexible daily tasks, stay in the notebook. That way, the calendar stays clean, and I'm not tied down to it. Items offloaded to the calendar will typically be crossed off the list.
